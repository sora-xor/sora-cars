# Tokyo 1986 — the palace loop's buildings and lighting

Research of 2026-11-11, checking what the Tokyo route draws against 1986. The route's geometry is the ledger table
and is unchanged (see [the world notes](../../README.md)); these are presentation facts. "Outer" is the driver's
right on the counter-clockwise loop, "palace" the left. Nothing here is surveyed.

## Corrections to what was drawn

- **Ministry of Justice red-brick building**: the roof burned in 1945; the 1948–50 repair changed its shape and
  replaced the slate with tile. The patterned slate roof and spires are 1991–94 restoration work, so they were not
  there in 1986 [1][2][3][4].
- **National Diet Building**: nightly floodlighting began on 28 Nov 1990; it was dark in 1986 [7].
- **Tokyo Tower**: outline bulbs only; whole-tower floodlighting began on 1 Jan 1989 [8].
- **Palace gates**: the permanent Kōkyo Gaien lighting was installed in FY2016–18; no evidence of floodlighting in
  1986 [9].
- **ARK Mori Building**: completed March 1986 (37 floors, 153 m), so it stands only after March [5].

## Confirmed standing in 1986

Yasukuni's first torii (rebuilt 1974 in weathering steel, pillars about 25 m, unpainted rust-brown) [6]; Kudan Kaikan
(1934) [12]; Budōkan (1964) [13]; Hanzōmon [14]; National Theatre (1966) [15]; Police HQ (June 1980) [16]; Sakuradamon
[17]; Nijūbashi (stone arch 1887, iron bridge rebuilt 1964) [18]; Dai-ichi Seimei (1938, ten square piers) [10]; Tokyo
Kaijō (1974, 25 floors, 99.7 m, red brick tile) [11]; Palace Side (1966, two cylindrical cores) [19]; the expressway
at Manaita-bashi (opened 30 Mar 1967) [20]; and on the skyline Kasumigaseki (1968), Akasaka Prince (1982), New Otani
tower (1974), Sunshine 60 (1978), the Shinjuku towers to the Hilton (1985), Fukoku Seimei (1980), Hibiya Kokusai
(1981), the Imperial Hotel tower (1983) and Nikolai Cathedral [21]–[29].

## Street details

- Toei buses: ivory with a green band from 1982 (older ivory and sky-blue buses still ran) [50].
- Taxis: the four majors ran lemon-yellow with a red band [51].
- Trees: cherries from Kudanzaka-ue to Hanzōmon [52]; pagoda trees at Ōtemon. Lamp and guard-rail types, and the
  police boxes at Hanzōmon and Iwaida-bashi, are unconfirmed.

## Not there in 1986

Shōwa-kan (1999); Kitanomaru Square (2006); the Justice Ministry tower block (1990); Hibiya Chanter (1987); DN Tower 21
(1993–95); Palace Hotel Tokyo (2012); the Tokyo Metropolitan Government building (1991).

## What the game now draws (2026-11-11)

- Landmarks placed by projecting their real positions (OpenStreetMap) onto the route; its line strays 40–220 m from
  the real streets, so places hold to a few tens of metres. Yasukuni's first torii, the stone lighthouse, the Budokan
  (200 m into Kitanomaru Park), Hotel Grand Palace (200 m behind Kudanshita), Police HQ (left) and the Ministry of
  Justice (right) on Sakurada-dōri, the Hibiya-dōri frontage in its real order, the Palace Hotel and Mitsui & Co.
- Open ground sampled from OpenStreetMap every 20 m along the loop: the forecourts at Hayabusachō, the Diet's front
  garden, ministries rather than a moat along Sakurada-dōri, and Hibiya Park.
- Corrections above (Justice roof, unlit Diet and palace gates); Tokyo Tower in the seven-band scheme of the 1986
  repaint with its main deck orange (the repaint's month is unconfirmed).
- Missing from the table above and still not drawn: the Indian Embassy, the FM Center, the Foreign and Finance
  ministries, Kensei Kinenkan, Tokyo Kaikan, the old Meteorological Agency, Gakushi Kaikan.
- The road table itself is unchanged (it is the ledger member): its heights stand within 2.6 m of the GSI model on
  average and 7 m at worst.
