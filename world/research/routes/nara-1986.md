## (a) Route and why this alignment

**The run, in compiled progress:**

1. **0 m**, start on old Sanjō-dōri just west of the JNR tracks.
2. **59 m**, across the JNR level crossing (Kansai Main Line / Sakurai Line), with the temple-roofed 1934 JNR Nara Station 21 m to the right at 74 m.
3. **155 m**, the station-front crossing.
4. Straight east up Sanjō-dōri, Nara's main street: shops, arcades, the 1926 Nanto Bank head office.
5. **1,045 m**, past Tarui-chō, where the documented one-way section ends. The road runs along the top of the bank above **Sarusawa-ike**: the pond is 5–8 m below on the right, and **Kōfuku-ji's five-storey pagoda** stands 49 m to the left.
6. Up the slope to **Ichi-no-torii** (1,444 m), Kasuga Taisha's vermilion first torii, 17 m to the right at the corner. Hard left.
7. North past Kōfuku-ji (left) and the Nara Park lawn and museum (right) to **Kenchō-higashi** (1,706 m).
8. Straight on as **Route 369**: prefectural office on the left; Tōdai-ji's woods and the Daibutsuden roof on the right. Over the Yoshiki-gawa bridge (2,130 m).
9. Right at **2,240 m** onto the lane along Tōdai-ji's north side past Kaidan-in.
10. Left at **2,427 m** beside **Daibutsu-ike**, with the Daibutsuden across the water. North along the Shōsō-in.
11. Right at **2,792 m** along its north side.
12. The **Shin-Wakakusayama toll gate** (3,164 m).
13. Fourteen hairpins:
    - five lower switchbacks through forest;
    - the **Mikasa Onsen** turning loop beside the hotels (4,219 m);
    - a stack of six hairpins;
    - **Jikkoku-dai**, the "ten-province" lookout and rest house (5,408 m).
14. A grassy **sky ridge** along the top of Wakakusa-yama to the **summit car park** (finish, 6,360 m). The view opens west over the whole Nara basin to the Ikoma ridge.

**Why this alignment:**

- **It uses only roads that 1986 evidence shows open to cars, in a legal direction.**
  - Sanjō-dōri's station section was **eastbound-only from December 1965** (8:00–21:00). The race runs east on a weekday before 21:00.
  - The climb is driven uphill from the north gate. That is legal whether the climb was two-way (today) or part of the one-way north→south through course.
  - Every road appears on the **1:25,000 "Nara" sheet (1983 second revision, published 30 May 1984)** and on the **GSI 1984–1986 aerial photographs**. I overlaid the OSM centreline and the compiled centreline on both.
- **It is a city run into a hill climb.** That suits a sprint, and it is what catalog entry 29 proposed: vehicle roads north of Nara Park → Shin-Wakakusayama Driveway → summit.
- **No legal loop exists.**
  - Sanjō-dōri cannot be driven west.
  - Beyond the summit, the driveway continues only as the one-way, mostly unpaved Kasuga Okuyama forest road (13 km through course).
  - A circuit would need an invented return leg.
- **It passes the brief's sites as closely as 1986 roads allow:**
  - JNR station (start);
  - Sarusawa-ike and the five-storey pagoda;
  - Nara Park and its deer;
  - Ichi-no-torii;
  - the National Museum (176 m, across the lawn);
  - the Daibutsuden (291 m, over Daibutsu-ike and from Route 369);
  - the Shōsō-in;
  - the driveways;
  - the summit.

**What does not fit, and why:**

- **Nandaimon** is 471 m from the nearest car road, inside the precinct, and is not visible.
- **The Kasuga lantern approach** is a pedestrian 参道 (restricted in OSM). It appears only as the view east through Ichi-no-torii at the corner.
- **The Nara Hotel** is 287 m away on its knoll beyond Ara-ike. Passing it would need a southward detour through Naramachi lanes whose 1986 one-way rules I could not verify, or a return leg.
- **The Kintetsu station and Gyōki fountain** are 235 m up the Higashimuki arcade, not visible from Sanjō-dōri. Taking 大宮通り/登大路 instead would lose the station, the pond and the pagoda.
- **The Heijō Palace site and Yakushi-ji** are 4–6 km west. Including them would break the 5:00 cap. They appear as summit views instead; see (d).

**Alternatives considered and rejected:**

| Variant | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Nara Park inner roads: 登大路 east → 春日野 → 手向山 → 二月堂 → behind the Daibutsuden | Today these are service roads in temple precincts and a one-way loop at the foot of Wakakusa-yama. I found no evidence of public through traffic in 1986. |
| Via Tegai-mon: Route 369 north to 転害門前, then right through 雑司町 residential streets to the Shōsō-in north road | Real and about the same length; it adds Tegai-mon (Nara period) on the right. The streets are narrower and their 1986 status is equally unconfirmed. A good swap if the lane past Daibutsu-ike proves private. |
| Full 奈良奥山 course | 13 km, one-way and mostly unpaved in the forest. Too long, and pedestrians share it. |
| Start at the Nara Hotel and run north on the Ichi-no-torii road | Loses JNR station, Sanjō-dōri, the pond and the pagoda close-up. |

## (f) Period details

**Road traffic** (typical 1986 Japan; general knowledge, not individually sourced):

- **Cars:** Crown S120, Mark II X70, Corolla E80 (including AE86), Skyline R31, Cedric/Gloria Y30, Bluebird U11, third-generation Civic, City, Familia BF.
- **Kei cars:** Alto, Mira.
- **Vans and trucks:** HiAce H50, Caravan, Carry / Hijet trucks.
- **Taxis:** black or company-coloured Crown and Cedric sedans with roof lamps.
- **Buses:**
  - **Nara Kōtsū** buses in the 1983 livery, with the leaping-deer emblem on the sides; colours unconfirmed.
  - Glass-roofed sightseeing bus from 1985.
  - **School-trip coaches**: autumn is school-trip season, and Nara is the classic destination.

**Rail:**

- JNR at grade at the start: the Kansai Main Line (electrified Nara–Minatomachi 1973) and the Sakurai Line, with crossing gates, bells and the 国鉄 station name board.
- Kintetsu runs underground (since 9 Dec 1969) and is not seen.

**Signage:**

- 国鉄奈良駅. Route 369 shields only from Kenchō-higashi northward.
- Blue guide signs to 春日大社 / 東大寺 / 若草山 (exact 1986 wording unconfirmed).
- A toll board at the gate.
- Deer warning signs plausible; 1986 design unconfirmed.
- No English-heavy modern tourist wayfinding.

**Sanjō-dōri shops:**

- Souvenir shops (deer goods, 奈良漬, 墨 and 筆, 一刀彫), cafés (喫茶), kimono shops, banks, ryokan near the pond.
- Arcade mouths of もちいどの and 東向.
- 鹿せんべい stalls by the park lawn.
- Afternoon, so signs are lit dimly or not at all; neon only on station-area hotels and bars.

**People:**

- Tourists with cameras, school-trip groups in gakuran and sailor uniforms with flag-carrying guides.
- Monks, shopkeepers, office workers near the prefectural office.
- The existing sprite cast covers most; school trips and tourists are the right emphasis.

**Deer:**

- Natural monument since 18 Sep 1957.
- On the park lawns, crossing the Ichi-no-torii road, along Daibutsu-ike and **on the summit grass**.
- In November the stags' antlers are already cut (the October 角きり), so show stumps.

**Season (late November):**

- Maple 紅葉 in Nara Park.
- **Golden ginkgo** around Daibutsu-ike and the Shōsō-in.
- Dark pines on the park lawns.
- Persimmons on trees by houses in 雑司町.
- **Wakakusa-yama grass dry gold-brown**; it is burned in January.

**SORA dressing:**

- The five breakaway SORA signs in `objects.json`.
- Cloth `SORA ⦿` banners across Sanjō-dōri and on shopping-street lamps (the period city's existing rules).
- Nobori before shops.
- The SORA airship holding over the climb.
- A SORA banner hung on the toll booth canopy.
- Always the exact emblem, centred.

## (g) Time, season, weather

**Thursday 20 November 1986**, start **about 16:05 JST**.

- **Weekday:** so Sanjō-dōri is in its eastbound-only hours (8:00–21:00), with no Sunday pedestrian closure. Sunday 23 Nov and Monday 24 Nov were the holiday and its substitute, hence a Thursday.
- **Sun** (computed with the NOAA approximation for 34.69 N, 135.84 E):
  - 8° above the horizon at azimuth 239° at 16:00, sinking to about 6°;
  - sunset 16:50.
- **Light along the route:**
  - On Sanjō-dōri the low sun is **behind the cars**: long shadows run ahead up the street and the pagoda's west face glows.
  - On the climb the light alternates between sun and shade.
  - At Jikkoku-dai and on the sky ridge, the basin opens west into gold, with the sun about 4° above the Ikoma–Shigi ridge.
- **Weather:** a brief late-autumn **時雨** (shigure) shower drifting over from the north-west during the climb, then a washed, clear **空色** sky at the ridge.
  - This is a creative choice suited to the season; I have not checked the real weather that day.
  - It maps onto the native weather window, which lowers grip from 1:00 to 4:00 of the race clock, as `precipitation: 'rain'` (light). That window covers roughly the toll gate to the finish.

**Distinct from Tokyo:** golden late-autumn afternoon into sunset, against Tokyo's rainy night to dawn.

- The model in `check.ts` gives **146.5 s**. It uses:

  - a 25 % speed loss whenever the car exceeds its steering limit.
- **Calibration against real AI runs.** `tools/calibrate.ts` runs the repo's test driver read-only on three routes already generated in `output/street-routes/`, each with eight cars on the native graph, and compares the same model:

  | irohazaka-1986 (hairpin sprint, 7.19 km) | 170.6 s | 172.4 s | 184.1 s |

  | aizu-wakamatsu-1986 (8.06 km) | 129.2 s | 148.3 s | 161.8 s |

## (i) Hairpins opened, 2026-10-29

Native steering cannot hold a path tighter than about 10.6 m at any speed, and the engine reads the road's heading once per 5 m cell. On the driveway the trace above compiled to bends of 6.7–9.5 m at 2.7–3.3 m half-widths. No car could steer the lower loop of the S-bend at 3,787 m at all. Before the change:

- a scripted driver that brakes to each bend's steerable speed scraped the wall in fourteen bends and took 207 s;
- the eight-car practice field logged 1,933 car-ticks of wall contact.

`scripts/street-open-bends.ts` applies `nara-1986.bends.json` to the trace, which is kept beside this note as `nara-1986.trace.json`:

| park-hairpin | drive-3441 to drive-3464 |
| mikasa-loop | drive-3541 to drive-3572 |
| s-upper | drive-3766 to drive-3790 |
| s-lower | drive-3818 to drive-3872 |
| onsen-turn | mikasa-onsen to drive-4302 |
| stack-upper | drive-4495 to drive-4526 |
| stack-lower | drive-4579 to drive-4612 |
| stack-turn | hairpin-stack to drive-4727 |
| forest-turn | drive-4813 to drive-4859 |
| upper-stack | drive-4920 to drive-4971 |
| jikkoku-turn | drive-5342 to drive-5372 |

| ridge-bend | drive-5581 to drive-5603 |
| summit-bend | drive-6359 and drive-6367 |

- **Bends.** Each is a shortest 13 m-radius (Dubins) path between anchors on the bend's own legs, resampled as control points about 5.5 m apart with the maximum trim. The Mikasa, S-bend upper and Jikkoku loops keep a bulb. The S-bend's lower loop leaves by a longer exit straight, so it stays 4 m clear of the next leg.
- **Corners.** Kaidan-in takes a 17 m trim, Daibutsu-ike and the Shōsō-in 18 m (all were 12 m).
- **Gates.** mikasa-onsen, hairpin-stack and jikkoku-dai moved 11.8, 5.5 and 16.2 m to the nearest new points.
- **Props.** hairpin-oil is now mikasa-onsen +170 m and jikkoku-planter is jikkoku-dai +50 m. Both stay on their original straights.
- **Grades and width.** Grades were eased back to 12% per edge, and no elevation moved more than 0.9 m. The driveway is 3.3 m half-width throughout.

**Result.**

- The route is 6.225 km, with 1,246 samples.
- Every bend allows at least 71 km/h on its widest line.
- The scripted driver takes the route in 151 s without touching a wall.
- The eight-car practice field finishes in 158.9–176.1 s (before: 167.1–180.6 s), with 1,515 car-ticks of wall contact.

Progress figures elsewhere in this note are the trace's (6.360 km).
