# Harbor Run — Yokohama's waterfront, Sakuragichō to Honmoku Pier, August 1986

Research of 2026-11-11 for the Arcade course's real setting. The course is 7.2 km (three 2.4 km laps drawn end to
end); course progress `p` is `100 + p` metres along the traced road (`yokohama-waterfront.road.json`): Sakuragichō
station, Honchō-dōri over Benten-bashi (p ≈ 270), Bankoku-bashi (965) onto Shinkō Pier and its red-brick warehouses
(1,445), back over Shinkō-bashi (1,595), past the Customs (1,875, back across the block on the right) to Kaigan-dōri
and the Prefectural Office (1,930), Ōsanbashi (2,134), Yamashita Park-dōri (2,190–3,030), Yamashita-bashi (3,100),
Shin-Yamashita under the Yamate bluff, a sharp left at Kominato (4,700), Honmoku Pier, and the Symbol Tower (7,439, in
the run-off). Past Bankoku-bashi the sea is on the driver's left. GSI has no 1984–86 aerial layer here; the 1974–78
and 1988–90 layers bracket the year. Nothing here is surveyed.

The first trace (from just past Bankoku-bashi, `startM` 345) was routed by length alone and ran down two side streets
and back (to the Customs, and at Yamashita-bashi) and along a residential lane parallel to Yamashita Park-dōri; that
was harmless while the course kept RaceV1's bends, not once it took the real ones. The second trace keeps to the main
roads and follows one-way carriageways; without its detours the road from Bankoku-bashi to D pier is 700 m shorter,
so the course starts at Sakuragichō instead. Today's Shinkō Pier roads (Bankoku-bashi-dōri, Kokusai-ōdōri) were
rebuilt for Minato Mirai and stand in for the pier's 1986 roads, which ran between sheds and rail sidings.

No freight line crossed Honchō-dōri. Route 82 through Shin-Yamashita (Yamashita-bashi 1961) and the road from
Kominato round to Honmoku Pier both existed; the Miharashi tunnel is a side link off the route. Not to be drawn for
1986: today's Sakuragichō station (1989) and the buildings round it, City Hall (2020), Landmark Tower (1993) and the
other Minato Mirai towers, Kisha-michi promenade (1997), World Porters (1999), the Bay Bridge, Honmoku JCT and the
Wangan line (1989), Route 357 on Honmoku (2016).

## What stood in August 1986

- **Bay Bridge: no towers, no deck.** Groundbreaking 27 Nov 1980, opened 27 Sep 1989. Caissons 1983–86, footings 1986
  to mid-1987, towers erected 1987. Visible in 1986: sea-level foundation platforms off Honmoku A pier, an access
  trestle and red-and-white floating cranes [1][2].
- **Symbol Tower**: completed 18 July 1986 on Honmoku D pier; white; 48.2 m (another source says 58.5 m); a
  ship-traffic signal station whose boards show I/O/F/X [3][4][5].
- **Hikawa Maru**: marine-blue hull with white in 1986 (black restored December 1988) [6][7].
- **Marine Tower** (1961, 106 m): seven red and white bands until 1989; its lighthouse flashed red and green every 20 s [8].
- **Ōsanbashi**: the two-storey passenger terminal of October 1964 [11].
- **Yamashita Rinkō freight line** (1965): from Shinkō Pier over the Shinkō-bashi rail truss, a viaduct past Customs and
  over the Ōsanbashi entrance, then a viaduct along the road-side edge of Yamashita Park to Yamashita Pier. Freight
  ended 1 Nov 1986, so it was still open; the park viaduct was removed in 2000 [13][14][16].
- **Shinkō Pier**: the red-brick warehouses (No. 2 1911, No. 1 1913) were bonded warehouses until 1989; rails ran in the
  roadway [17][18]. Shinkō-bashi: 1912 rail Warren truss beside the road bridge [19]. Bankoku-bashi: RC arch, 1940 [21].
  Minato Mirai 21 was an open reclamation site; Nippon Maru had been in her dock since 1985 [22].
- **Shuto Expressway**: the Kariba Line ran from Ishikawachō JCT to the Shin-Yamashita ramps (2 Feb 1984) on a viaduct
  over the Nakamura and Hori rivers; nothing beyond until 1989 [23][24]. Yamashita-bashi dates from 1961 [25].
- **Kaigan-dōri and Yamashita Park-dōri, driving south-east**: Customs "Queen" (1934, 51 m, verdigris dome) on the
  left; Prefectural Office "King" (1928) on the right; "Jack" (1917) about 200 m inland, its domes not restored until
  1989; Silk Center (1959) right at the Ōsanbashi entrance; then on the right the Trade Center (1975), Kenmin Hall
  (1975), Hotel New Grand (1927), Star Hotel (1984), Marine Tower and the Doll Museum (June 1986); on the left the
  park, the viaduct and Hikawa Maru. Past Yamashita-bashi on the right, the Bund Hotel (1929–1999) with its blue
  "Shell Room" neon [26][29][30][31].
- **Floodlighting**: "Light-up Yokohama" was first held in 1986 as an experiment on twelve Kannai buildings; the month
  is unconfirmed [32].
- **Shin-Yamashita**: the timber basin (1933) was still water until 1995–98 [33]. **Honmoku**: the US Navy housing was
  released in March 1982 and was cleared land under readjustment works [35]. **Honmoku Pier** (1963–70): gantry cranes
  on D pier, a red-orange crane at A pier [1][37].
- **Chinatown**: the main gate was the 1955 concrete gate with a 中華街 plaque (rebuilt 1989 as today's Zenrinmon)
  [39][40]. It is not on this route.

## Not there in August 1986

Bay Bridge (1989); Route 357 and the Kariba Line beyond Shin-Yamashita; Landmark Tower (1993) and all Minato Mirai
buildings; Red Brick Park (2002); the new Ōsanbashi terminal (2002); Zō-no-hana Park (2009); Police HQ tower (1991);
New Grand Tower (1991); the silver Marine Tower (2009); black Hikawa Maru (Dec 1988); Mycal Honmoku (1989); the
present Chinatown gates (1989–2003).
