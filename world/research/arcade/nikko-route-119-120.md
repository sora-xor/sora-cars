# Sakura Pass — Nikkō station to Kiyotaki by Routes 119 and 120, 1986

Research of 2026-11-11 for the Arcade course's real setting. The course is 5.4 km (three 1.8 km laps drawn end to
end); course progress `p` is `100 + p` metres along the traced road (`nikko-route-119-120.road.json`): the JNR station
(p ≈ 40), the main street, Shinkyō and Nikkō-bashi (1,830), Nishi-sandō (2,515), Tamozawa (3,050), Arasawa (4,567) and
the rise before Kiyotaki, where the line is (5,400); the run-off rolls down into Kiyotaki. It climbs from 532 m to
707 m. Sides are for driving west. Nothing here is surveyed.

The Tōbu tramway along this road closed on 25 Feb 1968; its abutments beside Shinkyō and its steel-arch bridges at
Tamozawa and Arasawa survived [27].

## Cherries and cedars

- **No cherry-lined road here.** The Nikkō-kaidō cherry avenue (planted 1951) lies far to the east, between
  Utsunomiya and Yamaguchi. On this road there are only single weeping cherries, such as the one inside the
  Tamozawa villa [28][29].
- The cedar avenue reaches Route 119 around Matsubara-chō from the east; cedars also stand at Shinkyō [30].

## Mountains (computed bearings; the street's axis is about 300°)

- Nyohō-san ahead to the right (314°), seen from the upper street; Akanagi-san to the right (330°).
- Nantai-san ahead to the left (280°), seen from the Tōbu forecourt.
- Nakimushi-yama (1,104 m) to the left, 2 km south across the Daiya.

## Not there in 1986

JR branding (1987); Tōbu's Spacia (1990); World Heritage signs (1999); Tamozawa memorial park (2000); the renewed
street with buried wires and dragon lamps (2003 on); the present Dainichi-bashi (2000).
