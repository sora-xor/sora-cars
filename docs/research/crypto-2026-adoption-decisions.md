**Cryptography through September 8, 2026: adoption decisions for Iroha games**

> Links to retired laboratory Rust implementations below now open the preserved removal snapshot. Recorded report hashes identify their original experimental revisions; these snapshots are historical source, not operational proof dependencies.


**Prioritize complete execution binding and efficient accumulation. Add FACS and ERA codes to the experimental shortlist.** Keep the implementation native Rust, with generic execution/game facilities and compiler-owned application relations. None of the research below requires adopting an external zkVM.

This is a focused update to the [earlier design review](crypto-2026-native-design-review.md), using primary records available by September 8. It covers verifiable computation, relevant cryptanalysis and authentication standards. Earlier research documents and proof evidence remain unchanged. Publication, preprint revision and implementation status are distinguished; external benchmarks and mathematical proofs have not been independently reproduced.

**Four additions to the shortlist**

| Work and available version | Supported advance | Proposed experiment and remaining obligation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **FICS/FACS**, May 20 revision matching CRYPTO 2026 | FACS accumulates NP claims with linear prover work and O(λ) oracle queries per update. FICS provides a related multilinear-evaluation proximity protocol. Their constructions use compatible codes and code-switching reductions. [Primary record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/737) | Compare FACS with WARP on complete ordered executions. Count update work, retained state, commitments, recursion and final decision. An oracle-query bound is not total verification cost. |
| **ERA codes**, May 3 manuscript, approved May 5 | Linear-time encoding over sufficiently large fields, with distance guarantees, code switching and an interleaved proximity protocol. The ePrint record still says preprint; an author lists ASIACRYPT 2026. [Paper record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/864), [author listing](https://pratyush.zip/) | Compare encoding and commitment costs for both native proof oracles while retaining Goldilocks/Fp4 as the initial workload. Establish the exact code parameters, extraction and opening reductions before claiming compatibility. |
| **Holography accumulation**, June 8 revision, listed for SCN 2026 | Separates witness-dependent verification from public polynomial checks for lincheck/checkable-subspace constructions, and accumulates the latter. [Primary record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/538) | Determine whether repeated compiler/read-map evaluations fit that decomposition. It does not automatically accumulate every check in our existing verifier. |
| **Query-optimal IOPPs**, March 13 revision, EUROCRYPT 2026 | Tensor-code reductions and lossy batching yield linear prover work and O(λ) queries. The revision corrects parameters and discusses practicality. [Primary record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1588) | Evaluate the complete construction and concrete constants. Its asymptotic bound cannot be substituted into our current WHIR query setting. |

These additions target two distinct costs: encoding large committed arrays and repeatedly proving the same computation. This separation is our design inference. Faster encoding alone cannot establish state continuity, and accumulation cannot establish who authorized an input.

The ERA authors link an implementation. Its pinned [Cargo manifest](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WizardOfMenlo/agnostir/f50d2d326c6e600cc541b29cbeb34735c0b428b7/Cargo.toml) uses Plonky3 interfaces and declares no package license. The review did not establish code-reuse permission, native Iroha compatibility or audited deployment status. It is algorithmic reference material at this stage. Full PDFs for the four additions were not available in the research lane; their detailed theorem constants and reductions remain reading requirements before implementation selection.

**Existing candidates remain relevant, with different trade-offs**

**WARP** remains a strong hash-based accumulation candidate: its June 18 revision gives linear prover time and logarithmic verifier time with unbounded depth in its model. The final decider and field costs still belong in the comparison. [Primary record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/753).

**LiftWHIR**, revised August 28, reports a 4.4–6.7× improvement for evaluation proving but only 1.55–1.61× for commitment plus proving in the reported benchmarks, with larger proofs and slower verification. Those results do not predict a whole racing prover's speed. [Primary record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1561). The [earlier review](crypto-2026-native-design-review.md) records the fast regime's conjectured security premises. **PIPFRI/DEPIPFRI**, published at USENIX Security in August, is another encoding/distribution candidate. Its polynomial sharding and worker accountability address computation distribution; they do not authenticate gameplay inputs. [Proceedings paper](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/usenixsecurity26-li-weihan.pdf).

**SuperNeo**, revised September 4, now supports extension-field CCS relations as well as general small-field constraints, using Ajtai commitments under Module-SIS. Evaluate it as a separately specified lattice-based alternative. Its folding message and a complete final proof are different artifacts. [Primary record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/242). **BinarySpartan**, revised September 3, is promising for binary/hash-heavy workloads; its CPU hash benchmarks do not establish an advantage for signed fixed-point driving. [Primary record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1656).

**September's coding-theory progress needs concrete instantiation**

The September 5 revision of **TR26-164** extends algorithmic Reed–Solomon list decoding toward capacity to all constant rates over prime fields and arbitrary evaluation sets. Its stated guarantees have field-size, slack and asymptotic conditions; polynomial time in the field size is not necessarily practical in its bit length. [Revision record](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/164/), [full revision, Corollaries 1.2 and 5.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/164/revision/1/download).

**TR26-169**, published September 6, additionally claims a mutual-correlated-agreement framework. Its abstract's line-error expression is n^{Oγ(1)}/q, rather than a ready numerical error bound for our parameters. [Primary record](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/169/). The [previous full-paper review](crypto-2026-native-design-review.md) records its extension-field characteristic conditions and unresolved practical constants. Do not replace field characteristic p with extension-field size p⁴ when checking those premises.

The earlier Crites–Stewart counterexamples still matter: they disprove specified broad capacity conjectures, rather than every FRI construction. New positive results must be matched to the precise code family and attack model. [Primary record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2046). Our adoption criterion remains an explicit, composed error budget for the actual protocol. A paper's “128-bit” benchmark setting is not that budget.

**Security findings that affect the implementation now**

Fenzi's August 31 preprint, approved September 1, attacks certain protocols whose concrete R1CS statements are generated from adversarial programs. Its mitigation binds the generated statement before the first Fiat–Shamir challenge, rather than merely hashing generator code. This is a scoped result, not a universal repair for all Fiat–Shamir constructions. [Primary record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1838). The CRYPTO 2026 functional-commitment composition result additionally requires state-restoration security and state-restoration function binding. Ordinary commitment binding is not its full premise. [Primary record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/902).

Our implementation consequence is to derive a relation identity from complete compiled sources, maps, genesis, carry layout, policy and codec semantics, and enforce that identity against the independently selected profile. Putting an expected identifier into otherwise unused transcript context is insufficient.

The [static review](../native-accepted-history-review.md) of the new [history bridge](../../output/qualification/unused-proof-mirror-retirement-20261018/preimage/world/native/accepted_guarded.rs) found two integration requirements still open: its generic constructor needs that compiled-relation comparison and an explicit genesis-root comparison; its archived mode needs the recorded outcome compared with the outcome projected from genuinely verified final outputs. The current CLI constructs its relation from fixed sources, which narrows the first concern on that path. These are source-review findings, not demonstrated attacks on a deployed chain. The new combined history adapter has not yet passed compilation or integrated proof tests.

Poseidon-family analysis also requires exact-instance reasoning. **Midpoint Reset** assumes an adaptively selected MDS matrix after round constants; **Skipping Class** improves attacks without thereby showing every affected parameter set falls below its claimed security. Neither paper alone demonstrates a break of our pinned instance. Joint parameter provenance and the complete sponge/Merkle construction remain review obligations. [Midpoint Reset](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1760), [Skipping Class](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/306), [Merkle construction analysis](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/089).

For authentication, NIST's August 5 status lists **ML-KEM, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA** as finalized, with Falcon and HQC standardization ongoing. Wallet/input signatures, Connect encryption and finality certificates need their own migration decisions. [NIST](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/post-quantum-cryptography). The August claimed DCP/lattice breakthrough must be read alongside the September 1 revised refutation; it does not establish that lattice standards are broken. [Refutation and linked Lean work](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1693). No Lean verification was run here.

**Implementation priorities**

1. Close the two bridge findings, then compile and adversarially exercise the complete accepted-history/guarded-proof path, including signed false checkpoints, changed compiled relations and wrong archived awards. Preserve partial batches and the state before same-tick removals.
2. Authenticate the selected ledger history, legal removal decisions and finalized inclusion. A self-consistent snapshot hash or valid input signatures cannot substitute for those facts.
3. Compare FACS/WARP accumulation and ERA/current encoding on the same short complete workload, then measure longer executions. Include both oracles, fresh inputs, final decision, total memory, disk/I/O, proof bytes and validator work. Add holographic decomposition only where its required structure is established.
4. Qualify the exact extraction, hash and Fiat–Shamir composition, supported targets and full-duration performance before admission. Public replays do not require a hiding proof; future private applications need a separately qualified zero-knowledge construction.

The latest completed [guarded-history baseline](../../world/native/NATIVE_GUARDED_HISTORY.md) has 26 tests and 21 accepted/70 rejected CLI cases. It proves short boundary/motion compositions, including zero-motion closure; it does not qualify full race duration, accepted-history authority, 128-bit composed security or settlement. The new accepted-history work and the broader SORA CARS goal remain incomplete. This study changed research priorities and recorded review findings; it did not change cryptographic parameters, dependencies or deployment state.
