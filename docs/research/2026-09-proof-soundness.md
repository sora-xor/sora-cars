# Native execution-proof soundness research — 7 September 2026

The useful direction is a theorem-backed parameter planner and an instance-specific hash review. Recent research improves several proven bounds, but it also disproves assumptions used by aggressive parameter sets. The current native scalar and staged verifiers remain useful foundations; their successful raw-witness tests do not establish a STARK or its 128-bit security target.

This review covers primary research available by **2026-09-07**, concentrating on 2024–2026. Dates below are publication or submission/revision dates shown by the primary repository, not search-engine crawl dates. An ePrint approval is archive admission, not peer review. “Theorem in a preprint” and “published theorem” are distinguished. Recent attack descriptions are the authors' reported results; this work did not independently run their cryptanalysis or validate all their complexity models. Some ePrint PDFs were unavailable through the research browser, so the latest attack entries conservatively report the primary abstract and metadata rather than claiming a full-paper audit.

## FRI and proximity gaps: the important change

For orientation, an RS code with rate `ρ` has a unique-decoding radius near `(1−ρ)/2` and a Johnson radius near `1−√ρ`. A proof using a theorem in one regime cannot silently substitute parameters from another. DEEP-FRI's published ITCS 2020 result uses out-of-domain evaluations to improve proximity testing and algebraic linking. It contains both proved results and a stronger conjectural direction; the protocol name alone does not select a soundness bound. [Published DEEP-FRI paper](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2020.5).

### The old up-to-capacity conjectures are false as stated

**Crites–Stewart, 2025-11-05; revised 2025-12-19; preprint.** The paper gives counterexamples to the original correlated-agreement, mutual-correlated-agreement, and DEEP-FRI list-decodability conjectures extending to capacity. It proposes modified conjectures and relates sufficiently strong correlated agreement to list decoding. A revised conjecture is still a conjecture. The practical consequence is to remove the falsified premises from a security calculator; this is not a demonstration that every FRI instance or deployed STARK is broken. [On Reed–Solomon Proximity Gaps Conjectures](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2046).

**Ben-Sasson–Carmon–Haböck–Kopparty–Saraf, ECCC publication 2025-11-07; ePrint received 2025-11-06; preprint.** Their positive results reduce the exceptional-challenge count to `O(n)` up to the Johnson radius, with zero proximity loss, improving the prior `O(n²)` dependence. Below unique decoding they obtain a constant exceptional count when allowing arbitrarily small positive proximity loss. They also give negative results beyond Johnson and a reduction to list-decodability. Constants and radius slack still matter; `O(n)` alone is not an executable security estimate. This is a promising source for improving our conservative planner without assuming capacity soundness. [ECCC TR25-169](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/169/), [ePrint record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2055).

**Kambiré, 2026-04-09, arXiv v1; preprint.** This report develops a Krachun–Kazanin sketch into a counterexample for a family over prime fields, at radii `O(1/log n)` below capacity. The lesson is relevant to prime-field designs: characteristic two is not the sole source of bad examples. It does not identify an attack on our particular Goldilocks domain, which has not yet been selected and analyzed. [Proximity Gaps Conjecture Fails Near Capacity over Prime Fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09724v1).

### Positive results require the right code family

**Goyal–Guruswami, ECCC 2025-11-06; revision 2026-03-24; preprint.** Their subspace-design approach establishes stronger proximity properties for folded RS, multiplicity, and certain random-code families, using line/curve decodability. The random-RS result concerns randomly chosen evaluation points. A fixed smooth multiplicative FFT subgroup is a different object; we cannot copy the bound while retaining that domain unchanged. [ECCC TR25-166 and revision](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/166/).

**Goyal–Guruswami–Sun–Wootters, 2026-07-09, arXiv v1; preprint.** The newer paper improves curve-decoding/proximity parameters for random ensembles, including random-evaluation RS, using a transfer from subspace-design codes. This advances the theoretical options, but again does not certify an arbitrary fixed Goldilocks FFT domain. A proposed replacement must preserve efficient encoding and state its random-code setup and failure probability. [Locality of Curve-Decoding and Improved Proximity Gaps](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08516v1).

**Chai–Fan, ePrint received 2026-05-02; preprint.** The current abstract of 2026/861 advertises an above-Johnson action–orbit result, but explicitly says general inputs depend on a sparse-worst-case dominance conjecture, Q2; only the sparse-input construction is described as unconditional. This is a research lead, not a general-adversary theorem we can use to qualify a ledger verifier. A formalization or benchmark of a restricted claim would not remove that premise. [Current primary record and stated qualification](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/861).

## Small fields, extension fields, and multiple tables

**Circle STARKs, received 2024-02-19; last listed revision 2025-02-20; preprint.** The circle construction enables efficient interpolation over fields whose `p+1` has a large power-of-two factor, notably M31. The authors' preliminary speedup is an implementation comparison, not a security proof for an arbitrary application. Moving to M31 is a new encoding/arithmetization project; our signed values and exact residual envelopes were derived for Goldilocks. [Circle STARKs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/278).

**S-two Whitepaper, received 2026-03-16; last listed revision 2026-03-24; preprint describing an implementation.** It formalizes flat AIR and proves cross-domain correlated agreement for multi-table circle FRI up to Johnson, separately discussing conjectural list/line-decoding regimes. This is directly useful if we replace the current dense layout with differently sized operation tables. It demonstrates why independently quoting one-table bounds is insufficient: possible nearby codewords must remain correlated across domains. The result must be adapted or replaced for our chosen code; adopting its table organization does not import its proof. [S-two Whitepaper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/532).

The following are **local mathematical/engineering deductions**, not claimed performance or security results from those papers:

- Let `p = 2^64 − 2^32 + 1` and `E = F_(p^4)`. A genuinely uniform extension-field challenge has about 256 bits of sample space. A nonzero degree-`D` polynomial has at most `D` roots, so the elementary test contributes at most `D/|E|`; restricting the sample set changes the denominator. Sampling only the constant coefficient of an `Fp4` object gives a base-field challenge, not extension-field soundness.
- A single base-field root bound of order `1/p` cannot by itself justify a `2^-128` statistical error bound. Repetitions or larger challenge fields need an actual composition analysis. Conversely, `Fp4` does not repair a false proximity premise, a weak hash, a missing equation, or an unbound public statement.
- The extension must be a field with a verified irreducible defining polynomial and canonical arithmetic/serialization. The verifier must derive the same challenge distribution as the proof assumes, including exclusions, rejection sampling and domain separation. Uniform elements of the entire extension can legitimately land in a proper subfield; do not introduce unanalysed exclusions as an improvised fix.
- Our witness-degree-two equations are not univariate degree-two AIR constraints. If columns interpolate with degree at most `N−1`, a product may reach `2(N−1)`; multiplying by a row selector of degree `N−1` may reach `3(N−1)` before quotienting. Actual activation sets, wraparound constraints, fixed coefficients and quotient divisors determine the bound. Changing stage/table organization changes this analysis.
- The current exact ROM uses compiled constants and retained Boolean/product/range equations. It adds no probabilistic lookup error today. Replacing it with a bus, permutation, or logarithmic-derivative lookup introduces a separate challenge and denominator analysis.
- Changing the scalar modulus to a 31-bit prime invalidates the current single-cell signed range representation and some no-modular-alias arguments. Limb arithmetic, carry constraints, and fresh worst-case envelopes would be necessary. Smaller cells do not imply lower whole-prover memory once extension columns and additional limbs are counted.

These deductions refer to the current local [scalar constraints](../../world/native/SCALAR_CONSTRAINTS.md) and [staging qualification](../../world/native/STAGED_CONSTRAINTS.md). They do not assess maintained Iroha code for compliance with the proposed protocol.

## Algebraic hashes: review the exact permutation and mode

The relevant recent evidence is broader than “an attack on Poseidon.” It includes improved solvers, weaknesses in some matrix choices, different attacks for compression versus sponge mode, and experiments on heavily reduced rounds. These require different responses.

The Ethereum Foundation's primary initiative page records recent reduced-round and partial-output challenges and distinguishes several field/width instances. For example, its historical Goldilocks Poseidon2 challenge uses width 8 and exponent 7; its newer KoalaBear targets differ. Those are challenge definitions and organizer-reported outcomes, not interchangeable security certifications for this project. [Poseidon Cryptanalysis Initiative](https://www.poseidon-initiative.info/home).

**Design inference:** freeze the complete hash instance in the compiled proof profile: field, S-box exponent, width, rate, capacity, digest size, round counts, constants, both linear layers, padding, mode, and domain tags. Publish parameter-generation provenance and test vectors. Treat Merkle compression, replay commitments, and Fiat–Shamir challenge derivation as distinct uses needing their own analysis. Do not optimize by generating a new “fast” MDS matrix or trimming rounds from a family-level claim. Keep a conventional-hash benchmark lane available so the recursive cost of algebraic hashing is an explicit tradeoff, rather than a requirement that forces premature cryptographic approval.

## Fiat–Shamir has its own obligations

**Khovratovich–Rothblum–Soukhanov, received 2025-01-25; published in CRYPTO 2025, first online 2025-08-17; ePrint revision 2025-12-11.** The paper constructs false accepting statements for a specific noninteractive GKR-based argument, with the circuit participating in the attack. This is a protocol/circuit-instantiation issue, not a generic break of every hash or FRI. It is especially relevant if a future generic execution framework accepts adversarial circuits or adds GKR lookup machinery. Fixed compiled relations help delimit review, but are not a complete defense theorem. [Published paper and date](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-01887-8_1), [revised manuscript](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/118).

**Chiesa–Orrù, received 2025-03-22; current revision 2026-03-27.** The current ePrint is explicitly a **major revision of a TCC 2025 publication**. It analyzes a duplex-sponge Fiat–Shamir transform in an ideal-permutation model, gives concrete bounds, and supplies the Rust `spongefish` implementation. It also explains why sponge indifferentiability alone does not establish the desired knowledge-soundness/zero-knowledge claims. This is a useful transcript design/reference path; it does not prove that a concrete Poseidon permutation is ideal. [A Fiat–Shamir Transformation From Duplex Sponges](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/536).

**Hovhannisyan–Asaturyan–Hovhannisyan, received 2026-02-27; approved 2026-03-02; preprint.** The authors jointly model reduced-round Poseidon challenge derivation and protocol checks. They report no meaningful forgeries in their experiments. This is negative experimental evidence for particular attack attempts, not a general security theorem. It does not cancel the separate attacks above. [Cryptanalysis of Poseidon-Based Fiat–Shamir Protocols](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/409).

## Concrete qualification work for Iroha execution profiles

The following is a proposed engineering checklist, not an already established bound:

1. **Define the target precisely.** State classical versus quantum adversary, knowledge versus decision soundness, allowed oracle work, and the interpretation of “128 bits.” A nominal 128-bit statistical IOP parameter or a nominal 256-bit digest does not alone specify the security of the noninteractive proof. Any quantum claim needs its own transform and hash analysis.
2. **Build a theorem-aware planner.** Pin the exact protocol and theorem/version, code family, evaluation domains, rates, list/curve bounds, extension field, all batch widths, and query/repetition strategy. Reject configurations relying on the disproved capacity statements. Keep conjectural research configurations visibly separate from deployable profiles.
3. **Compute actual degree and failure terms.** Account for every selector, quotient, boundary, cross-table link, DEEP sample and any new probabilistic lookup. Maintain a symbolic ledger of reduction errors; only after choosing a valid composition theorem turn it into a numeric bound. Do not add or multiply unrelated “security bits.”
4. **Specify the transcript byte-for-byte.** Bind network, compiled relation and tables, public state/input/output commitments, roster and authorized keys, rules, dispute history, and all round messages before their dependent challenges. Include lengths/types and protocol-domain tags. Audit rejection sampling, extension-coordinate sampling, grinding/retry limits, and commitment binding under the selected transform.
5. **Review the exact hash against the 2025–August 2026 attack set.** Separate experimental attacks, asymptotic models, and concrete security estimates. A promising parameter set remains unqualified until this work and independent cryptographic review are complete.
6. **Retain adversarial implementation evidence.** Test noncanonical extension encodings, coefficient permutations, base-field-only challenges, malformed/duplicate openings, altered profile/table commitments, dropped constraints, cross-table list inconsistencies, and cross-session transcript replay. These tests expose implementation defects; they do not replace the theorem or quantify cryptanalytic hardness.

Open questions are the actual univariate AIR/PCS chosen for the full race, its exact challenge schedule and theorem-compatible domains, the multi-table composition if introduced, the approved hash instance/modes, and classical/quantum target semantics. Native compilation, complete proof generation, full-duration resources, ledger binding and finality are separate gates. This document changes no source, no Iroha deployment, and no live-stake admission state.
