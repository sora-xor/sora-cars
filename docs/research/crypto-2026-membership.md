## A concrete current LogUp correction: denominator failures have their own cost

Plonky3's author-maintained [`security/src/logup.rs`](https://github.com/Plonky3/Plonky3/blob/7230fc572870436e6651762f35c6c3f3f48960d2/security/src/logup.rs) was checked at commit `7230fc572870436e6651762f35c6c3f3f48960d2`; its [patch](https://github.com/Plonky3/Plonky3/commit/7230fc572870436e6651762f35c6c3f3f48960d2.patch) is dated September 7, 2026. The file's SHA-256 is `0a4729b45e35842a80346d9b9059f983d3a7949b6c5b2715867abf6ae284da4e`. It accounts for both a cleared-numerator root and a vanishing denominator, giving `N(W+2)/|EF|`. A batched identity can lose its fraction constraint when its common denominator vanishes. The code also treats multiplicity no-wrap as a premise, and lookup error as an explicitly supplied composition term. This is inspected implementation documentation, not an independent proof of Plonky3's bound.

**Native consequence:** retain each `(beta − key) * inverse = 1` and reject every public-table pole, including rows with zero claimed multiplicity. Our separate namespace sums differ from Plonky3's compressed bus; copying its bound would be inappropriate. Any future product-denominator batching must either preserve nonzero constraints or account for its additional bad event. Challenge zero is allowed; a pole aborts without transcript retries.

The [Eagen–Haböck characteristic-bound note](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/2067) is a 2024 preprint revised **February 20, 2025**, not a new 2026 release. It generalizes pole multiplicity and includes binary-field fractional decomposition. **Native consequence:** ordinary positive-count membership remains suitable while each namespace count is below Goldilocks' characteristic. Sampling in Fp4 enlarges the challenge space but does not change that characteristic. Removing the count bound or moving ordinary multiplicities to characteristic two would require a different argument.

## Cheaper lookups are a protocol replacement, not omitted inverse rows

[Logup*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/946), received **May 24, 2025**, remains an informal preprint in the checked record. Its indexed-lookup construction avoids extra commitments as long as the indexing array and targets small tables; it also addresses numerator-overflow concerns. The previous study already inspected the October 2025 Powdr hash-commitment adaptation. **Next experiment:** retain all 24 native namespaces and exact table entries, but compare complete virtual-evaluation/GKR openings against the current inverse oracle. Count extra rounds, table commitments, address evaluation and peak live memory; the abstract alone does not specify an implementation.

The [Twist/Shout ePrint](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/105) still lists **February 27, 2025** as its latest revision, including corrections to local Twist and Booleanity cost accounting. The [author publication record](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/twist-and-shout-faster-memory-checking-arguments-via-one-hot/) identifies **CRYPTO 2026** and adds Michael Zhu to Setty and Thaler. **Native consequence:** use Shout's read-only component as a candidate for indexed ROM and fixed operand reads; this game relation does not need Twist's mutable RAM. Do not combine the older manuscript's estimates with the conference record's different implementation evidence.

## Small-field progress and the remaining base-field theorem

[Packed Sumcheck](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/719), received April 22 and last revised **October 3, 2025**, remains a preprint. Its current formulation combines repetition and folding while retaining round-by-round soundness over the small prime base field. This is a different protocol, not ordinary sumcheck with smaller challenges. The current nine-author revision also differs from cached versions carrying an FHE-specific title. **Native consequence:** compare its complete arithmetic and opening costs only after specifying the repeated/folded relation; it does not establish base-field membership of our extracted witness.

A useful **local algebraic lemma**, still requiring protocol qualification, is: if an extracted `f ∈ K[X]`, degree `< d`, agrees with a base-field codeword on at least `d` distinct points of `F`, its coefficients lie in `F`. Raising coefficients to the `p`th power gives another degree-`<d` polynomial agreeing at those points; their difference is zero. The existing exact basis conversion then puts Boolean evaluations in `F` too. This does **not** prove that the implemented PCS supplies that extracted polynomial before lookup challenges. Required premises include the exact proximity/extraction theorem, agreement threshold, base alphabet, compiler-OOD order and noninteractive composition.

The **July 7, 2026** revision of [Chmel–Hubáček–Stejskal](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/284) separates AGM knowledge soundness from stronger extractability notions. It concerns group-based PCS and is **not a WHIR attack**. Its relevance is terminological and compositional: authenticated evaluations, binding, and a prechallenge extracted witness are distinct guarantees. Keep the current extraction flags false until the native theorem connects them.

## Spartan developments and what to adopt

[BinarySpartan](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1656), received **August 11**, last revised **September 3, 2026**, is a preprint instantiating Spartan with binary fields, Ligerito and ring switching. The author reports CPU-only M4 Max hash benchmarks and a 6.2 ms proof for a 2 KiB SHA-256 message, including the paper's specified benchmark conditions. These are external hash-workload claims, not measurements of our physics or lookups. **Native consequence:** investigate its sumcheck/layout techniques, but a binary-field backend requires re-encoding integer carries, signed comparisons and membership. It is not a drop-in speedup for Goldilocks/Fp4.

The inspected [Spartan-WHIR README](https://github.com/ethereum/spartan-whir/blob/f525cddea38bb605304d3a8e6394dda10ac64b4a/README.md), at the September 1 pin already retained in the backend study, uses explicit split commitment/finalization and composed security accounting. Its supported target range is 80–123 bits; the published SHA-256 comparison uses 116 bits. **Native consequence:** retain phase-specific roots and uninterrupted transcript continuation, and account for lookup, outer sumcheck, every PCS, and all hash events. Neither its parameter labels nor its benchmark speed supplies our 128-bit target. No external implementation was built here.

Finally, the **September 1-approved** [generated-R1CS Fiat–Shamir attack](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1838) reinforces binding the actual compiled relation before the first challenge, as our new adapter does. The **March 2026** [ring-lookup preprint](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/471) separately demonstrates failures when field techniques are transferred to rings. Neither establishes an attack on this native adapter; both rule out treating a generic proof framework as independent of its relation encoding or algebraic domain.

The immediate sequence is therefore: qualify extraction and full composition; finish deferred adversarial evidence; instrument actual proving phases; then implement one fully authenticated Logup*/Shout comparison behind a separate versioned profile. Preserve complete equations, ports, namespaces, tuple columns and explicit padding throughout.
