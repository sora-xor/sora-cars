Limber deserves a bounded integer-arithmetic experiment, but does not presently justify replacing the native Goldilocks/Fp4 backend or weakening its constraints. Its full paper makes the range-check, field-size and composition obligations clearer than its abstract. No proof, benchmark or author implementation was executed during this review.

**What the construction actually requires.** Protocol 6.1 commits the integer witness and quotient polynomials before sampling the fingerprinting prime `p`. The integer mod-R1CS equation is `Az ◦ Bz = Cz + mods ◦ quos`. Limber's separate underlying PCS field has characteristic `q` and cardinality `|F|`. Confusing these two fields or sampling `p` before witness commitment loses the stated reduction's premise. Theorem 5.1 requires a knowledge-sound PCS and a perfectly complete batch range argument; its evaluation knowledge-error expression is `εPCS + εrc + 2l/p + εIntEvalArg`. The compilation theorem it invokes, Theorem 4.20, explicitly requires witness-extended emulation. Its asymptotic cost table excludes batch range-check costs. [Paper, pp. 19, 22–25, 32–35](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1635.pdf).

Theorem 5.5 states an IntEval error bound

`εIntEval = (32λm/P)^s + sm²/(k|F|)`

under `m ≤ λ`, `||g|| < T ≤ 2^λ`, a bound on the integer opening point, `log₂ P ≥ 5`, and the exact characteristic condition

`(q − P)/2 ≥ 2^k P^k max(T,P)`.

Lemma 5.7 then adds two range-check errors and an unspecified negligible compilation term. Theorem 6.9 and its proof add fingerprinting, sumcheck and sparse-evaluation errors; the proof displays `(32n + O(log T))/2^λ + εsparse`. Section 6.4's final argument discussion retains asymptotic error notation, and argues round-by-round soundness for Fiat–Shamir by component choices. It is not a complete numerical 128-bit bound for our execution protocol. We did not mechanically verify these proofs, their cited reductions, or a quantum-random-oracle instantiation. [Paper, pp. 25, 29–33, 40–41](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1635.pdf).

**A concrete Goldilocks admission check.** Table 3's final row uses `log₂ q=64`, `T=2^32`, `P=2^30`, `k=1`, `m=25`, `s=8`, and reports extra commitment data of 16 times the witness. Substituting those literal powers into Theorem 5.5 requires `q ≥ 2^64 + 2^30 = 18446744074783293440`. Native Goldilocks is `18446744069414584321`, so that row does **not** certify the native characteristic. Appendix A's logarithmic selector drops the `−P` correction and accepts this boundary. This is a derived failure to meet the printed sufficient condition, not an attack or a proof that Limber cannot operate over Goldilocks. Parameters need exact recalculation. Fp4 increases `|F|`, not `q`. [Paper, pp. 30–32, 49](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1635.pdf).

The retained [exact arithmetic check](../../tmp/limber-2026-09-22-research-work/arithmetic-check.json) additionally separates the coarse Theorem 5.5 estimate from Appendix A's different prime-count estimate. It does not claim to disprove the latter. Even the standalone `32n/2^λ` contribution at `n=25, λ=128` gives only about 118.36 bits before other errors. A parameter called 128 cannot be treated as 128-bit composed security.

**The September implementation is different evidence.** The author's [repository](https://github.com/lucasxia01/limber-impl/tree/8c03d77d287148e0d607445b473e3d0ae581ad3c) was found and pinned at `8c03d77d287148e0d607445b473e3d0ae581ad3c` (September 17). The paper reports a Hyrax/Tom256 implementation and a 6,209-constraint RSA case; its Zinc+ comparator uses unwired mock constraints. The pinned README instead describes a fully wired 12,796-constraint case, additional Brakedown results and changed baseline/security settings. Those author reports must stay separate; neither establishes an Iroha speedup. [August paper, pp. 3, 41–43](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1635.pdf), [pinned README](https://github.com/lucasxia01/limber-impl/blob/8c03d77d287148e0d607445b473e3d0ae581ad3c/README.md).

Selected source inspection found `LAMBDA_BOUND2=117`, with comments explicitly distinguishing it from `LAMBDA=128` and describing an approximately 114-bit fingerprint contribution. Parameter validation uses characteristic bit width and an approximate partial-norm check; this is not a ready Goldilocks/Fp4 parameter validator. The driver visibly absorbs the verifier-key digest, witness/quotient commitments and public input before prime sampling. The sampler fixes the runtime prime width at 128 bits. These are source observations, not a complete transcript, sampling or security audit. [Parameter source](https://github.com/lucasxia01/limber-impl/blob/8c03d77d287148e0d607445b473e3d0ae581ad3c/src/provider/pcs/integer_modpcs.rs), [driver](https://github.com/lucasxia01/limber-impl/blob/8c03d77d287148e0d607445b473e3d0ae581ad3c/src/imod_spartan_modp.rs), [sampler](https://github.com/lucasxia01/limber-impl/blob/8c03d77d287148e0d607445b473e3d0ae581ad3c/src/prime_sampler.rs).

**Fit to signed fixed-point racing.** Remark 5.11 handles bounded negative polynomial values by shifting by `Tf` and subtracting the shift at evaluation; it does not specify our simulator's signed division, tie rounding, saturation, comparisons or branch semantics. These still need explicit constrained encodings and bounds, including intermediate quotients and collision ordering. The author's 32/64-bit example proves a wired multiplication chain *modulo* `2^w`, not bounded non-wrapping signed physics; it also prints a size including analytically counted sumcheck bytes, rather than a complete serialized transport proof. [Paper, p. 34](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1635.pdf), [example source](https://github.com/lucasxia01/limber-impl/blob/8c03d77d287148e0d607445b473e3d0ae581ad3c/examples/int_mult.rs).

Recommended next experiment: preserve one frozen native transition relation and compare exact multiply/divide/remainder/range-check subrelations, then the complete accepted segment, with signed-boundary parity and malformed-input rejection. Count limb commitments, range proofs, prime sampling, sparse lookups, all openings, actual serialized proof bytes and full verifier work. Recalculate exact characteristic and total security bounds first. Limber is not an accumulation protocol and does not authenticate gameplay authorization, dispute decisions, finalized inclusion or settlement. The paper's coprocessor model is explicitly an informal sketch; using it requires a separate sound composition. Public replay removes the need for witness privacy, not these execution obligations.
