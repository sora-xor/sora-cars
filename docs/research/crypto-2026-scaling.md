**Scaling native execution proofs — research cutoff September 8, 2026**

The strongest new directions are hash-based accumulation for complete execution shards, September's SuperNeo extension-field folding, and reducing intermediate commitment work with GKR and structured sumcheck. They address different costs. None directly turns the current one-tick component into a complete race proof or establishes its required 128-bit security target.

This is a focused update to the [earlier survey](2026-09-08-cryptography-update.md) and [memory study](2026-09-08-memory-proofs-notes.md). It distinguishes paper revisions, conference status and repository observations. Paper metadata/abstracts, author code documentation and the cited SWIRL/DATE manuscripts were inspected; external benchmarks were not reproduced. No production code, dependency or Iroha build changed during this study. Recommendations below are our design inferences, not claims that these papers implement our relation.

**The measured workload**

The finalized [native membership report](../../output/qualification/native-membership-street-qualified/report.json), SHA-256 `b195ac0182677306d62bc965efb0322021e9494e2d7b1e753a1ed266f2383c2c`, proves the scalar and membership equations of the complete retained one-tick fixture, with public ports, both oracle tails and real native commitments. The verifier runs separately with a public statement and proof; the original witness path is absent.

The CLI total includes source construction, setup and initial commitment; adapter time includes several proof phases and is **not a sumcheck-only measurement**. Eight cars have 702,813 equations, 219,236 range occurrences, 640 ROM occurrences, and six factors of 2,097,152 extension elements: **384 MiB of dense factor payload**. Phase-0/phase-1 value domains are 524,288/262,144; their first codeword domains are 8,388,608 base elements and 4,194,304 extension elements. Commitments, compiler structures and scratch explain why optimizing factor buffers alone cannot eliminate whole-process memory.

These are component results. Base-field extraction/membership theorems, full integer execution, accepted history, composed security and ledger finality remain explicitly unqualified. A counterfactual concatenation of 5,400 identical eight-car proofs would consume 6,407,316,000 bytes and 93.13 sequential proving hours. That is arithmetic on a one-tick measurement, **not a measured full race or a prediction after batching**; serial memory does not multiply by tick count.

**Accumulation and folding: the important updates**

**WARP / Linear-Time Accumulation Schemes** was received April 27, 2025, published at TCC 2025, and substantially revised June 18, 2026. It provides linear prover time and logarithmic verifier time, hash-based security in the random-oracle model, and unbounded accumulation depth. Its proximity reduction supports linear codes over sufficiently large fields; extraction uses erasure correction. This is the most directly relevant accumulation candidate for retaining a transparent commitment architecture. An [author Rust implementation](https://github.com/compsec-epfl/warp) exists; its presence is not an audit or our workload benchmark. [Current paper record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/753).

**Arc**, a CRYPTO 2025 publication revised June 5, 2025, is the Reed–Solomon-specific comparison. It accumulates proximity claims without a fixed depth bound and also gives direct polynomial-constraint accumulation. Its code-rate-dependent Merkle work is relevant to our WHIR domains. Its list-decoding claims must be instantiated under the applicable theorem and parameters; they cannot replace our soundness calculation. We have not measured either Arc's recursive verifier circuit or WARP's final decision cost for these two-phase equations. [Paper record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1731).

**Neo and SuperNeo**, CRYPTO 2026, has a material **September 4, 2026 revision**, superseding Neo 2025/294. SuperNeo removes Neo's SIMD constraint-system restriction and supports CCS over arbitrary extensions of the underlying Ajtai field, without an NTT embedding. It combines small-field sumcheck with pay-per-bit Ajtai commitments under **Module-SIS**. This substantially improves its fit to native Goldilocks/Fp4 computations. It also changes the commitment assumptions and requires compatible norm bounds, parameters and a final decider; it is not an interchangeable WHIR opening. No reproducible end-to-end SuperNeo benchmark for our field/relation was established in this study. [Revision and publication record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/242).

**Symphony**, revised August 20, 2026, is listed for ASIACRYPT 2026, which is forthcoming at this cutoff. Its lattice-based high-arity folding aggregates many statements in one step; a generic compiler avoids embedding Fiat–Shamir hash circuits into the proved statements. Its low-memory prover uses O(log log n) input passes and is dominated by witness commitments. This offers a whole-history architecture comparison when recursive hash circuits are costly. It introduces a separate lattice/composition qualification task; asymptotic passes do not establish local RAM, proof size or speed. [Paper record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1905), [author publication listing](https://chancharles92.github.io/).

**GKR, memory and accelerated kernels**

SWIRL's March 13, 2026 manuscript describes LogUp-GKR multiset checks without additional witness commitments and stacks polynomials with different domains before WHIR. The concrete question now is whether a native interaction reduction can avoid our separately committed inverse bank while preserving every range/ROM occurrence. It requires the entire reduction and authenticated terminal checks; deleting inverse equations is not an optimization. This builds on the prior survey rather than introducing an external VM. [Manuscript, §§3.4–3.7](https://openvm.dev/swirl.pdf).

The February 25, 2026 revision of **Time-Space Trade-Offs for Sumcheck** gives a product-sumcheck algorithm using O(N(log log N + k)) time and O(N^(1/k)) space. Its reported up-to-120-fold memory reduction with less than twice the runtime concerns the evaluated sumcheck algorithm. That does not promise a 120-fold reduction in our 1.57 GB process. Structured blocking is nevertheless a stronger candidate than repeatedly recomputing every folded value from the original source. [TCC 2025 paper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1473).

**Speeding Up Sum-Check Proving**, revised June 17, 2025 and listed as a preprint, exploits small values/subfields and equality-factored polynomials. Its reported 2–3× gain concerns Jolt's first Spartan sumcheck, not an entire prover. Native base-by-extension kernels are worth testing; our flattened terms require a justified algebraic mapping before applying equality-factor tricks. [Paper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1117).

The author-maintained **efficient-sumcheck** Rust repository, observed September 8, advertises streaming, MSB/LSB variants, NEON and AVX-512 kernels. Its listed Goldilocks fast paths cover degree-2/3 extensions, not our Fp4. Its security policy explicitly calls it unaudited research software and leaves terminal oracle authentication to the caller. No immutable revision was established here; this is implementation evidence to investigate, not a pinned dependency recommendation. [Repository](https://github.com/compsec-epfl/efficient-sumcheck), [security policy](https://github.com/compsec-epfl/efficient-sumcheck/blob/main/SECURITY.md).

**SumCATS**, DATE 2026, reports 1.81× on RTX 3090 Ti and 1.62× on A100 over Binius-GPU for its largest binary-tower sumcheck workload. It reduces global memory traffic and delays extension operations. These are useful implementation ideas, but binary fields, CUDA devices and a GPU-kernel baseline differ from native Goldilocks/Fp4 and total CLI cost. They establish no speedup on this Mac or our prover. [Conference paper](https://past.date-conference.com/proceedings-archive/2026/DATA/926.pdf).

**Five actionable recommendations**

1. **Define complete ordered shards before accumulation.** Bind network, compiled rules, race, accepted inputs, dispute epoch, start/end state, tick interval and first-terminal behavior. Preserve the commitment-before-challenge order of multiplicities, compression challenges and inverse advice. Test reordered, omitted and duplicated shards, mismatched state boundaries and alternate accepted histories. A collection of valid independent tick proofs does not establish their composition.
2. **Compare WARP/Arc with one final decision first; keep lattice folding separate.** Measure accumulated state, update work, verifier/decider circuit size and final proof for the same complete short history. SuperNeo's September revision merits a separately reviewed native Module-SIS experiment; preserve the existing hash-based baseline for a fair comparison.
3. **Profile and cache immutable compilation, then implement bounded sumcheck.** Separate source compilation, relation preprocessing, initial/auxiliary encoding, sumcheck, Merkle hashing and openings. Compare blocked proving on the exact six-factor workload, recording process RSS and I/O as well as buffer estimates. Require scalar/native accelerated transcript parity; do not multiply a kernel headline by the entire 62-second CLI time.
4. **Evaluate GKR against the whole membership adapter.** Count any saved inverse commitment against new layers, public-table evaluation, opening reductions and soundness losses. Keep aliases, constant operands, zero-multiplicity tables, poles and both tails in the comparison. Reducing committed advice may help memory and proof size together.
5. **Qualify the final architecture before hardware expansion.** Run progressively longer complete histories with deterministic fixtures, separate verifier processes, real commitments and adversarial boundaries. Report end-to-end costs and security assumptions for each version. Then port measured hot kernels to supported native SIMD/GPU targets, including transfer and memory costs. No folding paper, hardware result or passing component test supplies the missing 128-bit composed analysis or finalized ledger verification.
