# Native backend decision notes — September 8, 2026

**Recommendation: compare heterogeneous AIR with a sparse sumcheck backend on the same complete, compiler-owned Street relation. Keep native Goldilocks/Fp4 as the initial arithmetic lane. Neither the WHIR paper nor the inspected Spartan implementation establishes the required 128-bit Iroha profile.** This is an architecture assessment; no external backend was built, benchmarked locally or adopted.

## What the primary sources establish

**WHIR — paper result.** WHIR is a proximity protocol for constrained Reed–Solomon codes, supporting queries to both multilinear and univariate polynomials. The authors also construct a generalized-R1CS SNARG. Thus WHIR is relevant to both candidate representations. Its fast polynomial-opening verifier is not a measurement of a complete racing proof. [WHIR paper record](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1586).

**SWIRL — manuscript architecture and conditional analysis.** The retrieved manuscript is dated March 13, 2026. It supports multiple AIR components with different trace dimensions, stacks their polynomial representations, uses LogUp-GKR for interactions, and reduces the resulting claims to WHIR. Sections 4.2.4–4.2.6 account for codeword batching, candidate-list factors, the chosen WHIR proximity regime and the Fiat–Shamir random-oracle query budget. Its quantum-random-oracle theorem is conditional on those bounds and model. These are paper claims, not native Iroha benchmarks or a concrete production security qualification. [SWIRL manuscript](https://openvm.dev/swirl.pdf).

**Spartan-WHIR — pinned implementation limits.** At commit `f525cddea38bb605304d3a8e6394dda10ac64b4a`, `src/security.rs` sets supported requested targets to 80–123 bits. Its documented upper limit comes from an eight-element KoalaBear Poseidon digest; the code explicitly leaves a wider digest and compression construction as work needed before supporting 128. The default soundness-assumption enum is `CapacityBound`. A caller-selected number or a larger extension alone therefore cannot establish our security target. [Pinned security source](https://github.com/ethereum/spartan-whir/blob/f525cddea38bb605304d3a8e6394dda10ac64b4a/src/security.rs).

**Spartan-WHIR — repository measurements, not measurements reproduced here.** The pinned README compares a 2,048-byte SHA-256 workload on an M4 Pro at a 116-bit target. Its no-ZK DirectSparse row reports 46.746 ms witness-plus-proving and 31.910 ms verification; no-ZK Spark reports 789.761 ms and 22.573 ms respectively. Those results use KoalaBear and a quintic extension, with different matrix-closing work. They do not measure our field, relation, lookups, shard boundaries or a 128-bit configuration. [Pinned README](https://github.com/ethereum/spartan-whir/blob/f525cddea38bb605304d3a8e6394dda10ac64b4a/README.md).

**Spartan-WHIR — implementation versus plan.** The pinned design document positions DirectSparse as a small correctness oracle and identifies SPARK as its scalable sparse-matrix closing path. It defers Spartan-LC. Both matrix modes are bound to keys and the transcript; a mode mismatch is rejected. [Pinned matrix-closing design](https://github.com/ethereum/spartan-whir/blob/f525cddea38bb605304d3a8e6394dda10ac64b4a/SPARTAN_LC_UPGRADE_PLAN.md).

## Implications for our native implementation

The following are **local engineering inferences**, not published speedup or soundness claims.

- **Heterogeneous AIR is the more direct comparison candidate for the current Street workload.** Keep arithmetic, exact range membership, fixed ROM reads, and authenticated operand/state interactions as explicit components. This can avoid forcing all tables into one largest rectangular trace, but the complete interaction, stacking, rotation and opening reductions remain real implementation and security work.
- **Sparse sumcheck is a serious alternative to serializing every scalar term into a univariate row.** It can address the sparse equation structure directly. However, our general sums of quadratic terms are not automatically rank-one constraints. Any added products, sparse matrices and matrix-closing argument must be counted, and exact range/ROM predicates still need proofs. A fast direct sparse verifier over all matrix entries does not establish acceptable validator cost at full-race size.
- **Compare complete bounded shards first.** Include immutable computation and table identity, all accepted action/state/final equalities, exact first-terminal policy, each range/ROM occurrence, fixed address namespaces and transcript-bound shard order. Compare actual prover memory, proof bytes and validator work. A small WHIR opening benchmark does not resolve the current full-race representation cost.
- **Select a whole versioned protocol after measurement and analysis.** Reusing an inspected architecture requires native implementations of its actual polynomial identities, degree accounting, challenge order, commitment construction and composed security budget. It does not authorize substituting KoalaBear arithmetic or copying another project's security setting into Iroha.
