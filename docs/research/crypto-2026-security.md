# Cryptographic security update — 8 September 2026

This targeted update supplements the [earlier survey](2026-09-08-cryptography-update.md). It prioritizes missing implementation lessons, exact hash-instance applicability, and post-quantum boundaries. Publication/revision dates below come from primary records or documents, not search crawl dates. New public-source snapshots are retained in the [source manifest](../../output/research/crypto-2026-security/sources.json). This is research and read-only source inspection; it changes no proof implementation, dependency, Iroha state, or security qualification.

## Soundness requires the right composition and transcript

**Functional commitments now have a more relevant composition theorem.** Chiesa–Guan–Knabenhans–Yu, ePrint **2025/902**, received **20 May 2025**, revised **2 June 2026**, associated with **CRYPTO 2026**, proves security of the functional-IOP → functional-commitment → Fiat–Shamir pipeline under **state-restoration security of the FIOP and state-restoration function binding of the commitment**. Ordinary binding is not the stated premise. For our linear-query openings, the actionable step is to identify which exact theorem establishes the required property of the native opening protocol and its two-phase composition. The paper’s Plonk instantiation does not itself certify WHIR or our implementation. [Primary record and paper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/902).

The **27 March 2026 revision** of Chiesa–Orrù’s duplex-sponge Fiat–Shamir analysis supplies concrete bounds in an ideal-permutation model and explains why ordinary indifferentiability alone does not establish knowledge soundness/zero knowledge. It lists quantum security of its transformation as an open problem. The separate **25 March 2026 revision** of **Feistel Tools** establishes **adaptive zero knowledge** for this transformation in the quantum random-permutation model, including preprocessing adversaries; that result must not be relabeled quantum soundness. The exact construction, security property and reduction loss all matter. [Duplex-sponge paper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/536), [Feistel Tools](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/146).

The CFRG Fiat–Shamir draft **03**, dated **17 August 2026** (datatracker update **16 August**), remains a work-in-progress Internet-Draft. Its codecs, session/instance binding and malformed-input vectors are useful review material. Its quantum discussion does not independently instantiate our framed Goldilocks permutation as a quantum random oracle. [Version-pinned draft, §§5–6 and 8](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-irtf-cfrg-fiat-shamir/03/).

**Two concrete failures sharpen the tests we need.** Solana’s **25 June 2025** postmortem and the discoverer’s **26 June** technical account describe a prover-chosen OR-proof component omitted before a later equation-batching challenge. Individual-looking algebraic pieces could consequently be combined into a false accepted proof; no in-the-wild exploit was reported. This is a transcript-order implementation failure, distinct from the generated-relation attacks already surveyed. Test every prover-controlled root, response, query claim and branch identity at the point where later randomness depends on it. [Solana Foundation](https://solana.com/news/post-mortem-june-25-2025), [discoverer’s account](https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/solana-phantom-challenge-bug/).

The **5 May 2026** Inferno disclosure reports repeated squaring where successive ordinary challenge powers were intended. Over its binary extension field, the resulting Frobenius-linearized check no longer had the claimed polynomial degree/soundness. This is not an attack on Goldilocks; it is a reason to compare actual challenge exponent schedules against the algebra rather than test only honest roundtrips. [Discoverer’s analysis and linked issue](https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/powers-of-funbenius/).

**Sampling corrections must also be read carefully.** Plonky3 PR **1598**, merged **30 April 2026**, changes WHIR query sampling to unbiased rejection sampling. Its later correction explicitly retracts the claim that deduplicating repeated opening positions necessarily weakens the exponent: the bound concerns the original independent uniform draws. Our sampling specification must preserve that distinction between draws and packed opening storage. The PR’s initial explanation is not reliable without its correction. [Primary PR, especially correction commit `de2beb1`](https://github.com/Plonky3/Plonky3/pull/1598).

## The exact Poseidon2 instance still needs review

The current native [provenance record](../../../iroha/crates/iroha_core/src/execution_proofs/POSEIDON2_PROVENANCE.md) specifies Goldilocks, width 16, exponent 7, eight full and 22 partial rounds, rate/capacity 8/8, six output coordinates, fixed Plonky3 matrices/constants, and a framed sponge. Known-answer vectors establish implementation agreement, not cryptanalytic resistance.

**Skipping Class**, received **18 February 2026**, is directly relevant: §6.1 specifically discusses Plonky3’s **64-bit-field, width-16** parameters and increased susceptibility to its round-skipping methods. Yet the authors report no examined instance falling below its asserted 128-bit level under their model. Their large relative speedup is not a 106-bit loss from 128-bit security. This justifies a mode-specific analysis of our exact capacity, output length, matrix and round schedule; it neither proves our instance broken nor certifies it safe. [Paper, §§5–6](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/306).

The **24 August 2026** nonlinear-subspace preprint reports stronger algebraic models. Its Table C.1 fixes **six full rounds**, capacity/digest chosen from its stated target, and uses **exponent 3** for the approximately 64-bit example. Those numerical rows cannot be transplanted to our different instance. The **21 August Midpoint Reset** preprint instead attacks original KoalaBear Poseidon with an **adaptively chosen MDS matrix**, after constants are fixed. Preserve joint parameter provenance and reject caller-selected hash matrices; neither paper supports silently changing native rounds or matrices. [Nonlinear subspaces, §4.2 and Appendix C](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1792), [Midpoint Reset](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1760).

## Post-quantum standards and the actual wallet boundary

NIST’s project overview, updated **5 August 2026**, lists **ML-KEM, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA** as finalized standards and **Falcon/FN-DSA and HQC** as ongoing standardization. HQC is a key-encapsulation mechanism, not a transaction-signature scheme. FIPS 204’s **31 July 2026** planning note identifies minor potential corrections; implementations should track the official errata. [NIST status](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/post-quantum-cryptography), [FIPS 204](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final).

**HAWK is a material new caution.** NIST confirms the **28 July 2026** vulnerability disclosure and the candidate’s subsequent withdrawal, while explicitly distinguishing the finalized standards. The authors’ key-recovery paper reduces the relevant SVP dimension, gives lower estimated costs, and demonstrates recovery for HAWK-256; it says the construction does not transfer to Falcon. Candidate selection is not equivalent to standardization or deployment qualification. [NIST notice](https://www.nist.gov/pqc), [authors’ paper](https://anthropic.com/document/hawk_key_recovery.pdf).

NIST’s **13 April 2026 initial public draft SP 800-230** proposes smaller/faster SLH-DSA parameter sets for limited-signature use cases, requiring no more than **2²⁴ signatures per key**. This is a draft with an explicit lifetime budget, not permission to substitute its parameters into an unrestricted validator or gameplay signing loop. [Primary draft, §3](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-230.ipd.pdf).

Read-only inspection finds a concrete migration boundary: [Connect browser code](../../../iroha/javascript/iroha_js/src/connect.browser.js) uses X25519/HKDF and accepts Ed25519 approval/transaction signatures; [game input authorization](../../src/network/session.ts) also uses Ed25519. The [Rust crypto library](../../../iroha/crates/iroha_crypto/src/lib.rs) exposes ML-DSA-65, but that alone does not extend these browser protocols. No live Taira configuration was inspected in this study.

Keep four claims separate: **proof soundness**, **account/game-input signature authenticity**, **session confidentiality**, and **finalized-ledger certificate authenticity**. A quantum-resistant proof of a calculation cannot repair forged classical authorization or a forged finality certificate. “128-bit classical,” “128-bit quantum,” and “NIST category 1” are also different targets: NIST defines categories against reference attack resources, not one universal bit counter. Larger hash output or an Fp4 challenge does not supply the missing quantum reduction. [NIST security-category definitions](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/post-quantum-cryptography/post-quantum-cryptography-standardization/evaluation-criteria/security-%28evaluation-criteria%29).

## Five implementation priorities

1. **Qualify the composed transcript.** Bind emitted relations and public context before challenges; document both commitment phases and indexed opening branches. Preserve explicit state-restoration/extraction assumptions and the adversary-query losses.
2. **Check malicious algebra, not only valid proofs.** Retain coherent false-witness tests, all six read/constant-offset mappings, exponent-schedule checks, and exact uniform sampling with repeated draws retained logically.
3. **Commission exact-instance hash analysis.** Use the pinned Goldilocks Poseidon2 sponge and the 2026 mode-specific attacks as the review input. Do not optimize parameters before that assessment.
4. **Specify wallet and consensus migration separately.** Evaluate standardized ML-DSA/SLH-DSA for authentication and standardized KEMs for session establishment, with explicit algorithm binding, key rotation, downgrade rules, wire sizes and signing limits. Audit actual finality certificates rather than infer their status from crypto-library availability.
5. **Keep qualification labels precise.** Report classical and quantum assumptions separately, including hash/Fiat–Shamir/PCS/lookup composition. Functional tests, transparent setup and current preprints do not by themselves justify a composed 128-bit or post-quantum claim.
