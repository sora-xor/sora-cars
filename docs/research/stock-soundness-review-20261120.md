## One set of rows through every commitment

Let D have N points, let H have n points and let Q denote the size of the extension field. Use m = 128 and let a be the ceiling of N times sqrt((n+2)/N) times (1+1/(2m)). Set the integer query cutoff to a−1. Define a list at a commitment prefix as the tuples of degree at most n polynomials jointly matching every column committed so far on at least a of the original rows. Distinct tuples agree on at most n rows. Counting pairwise intersections bounds the joint list by the floor of N(a−n)/(a²−Nn). Projecting any tuple to an earlier commitment keeps its common rows. The exact native size bound is 227 tuples for each 8,192-row Stock shape. [Joint list and rational discrepancy](../../output/reviews/live-staking-qualification-20261120/protocol/README.md)

In the oracle model a fixed full transcript defines a set P of passing positions. A single query must match the virtual DEEP value to the corresponding slot of the first committed FRI leaf, match each subsequent committed slot to the preceding fold, and end at the final polynomial. All query openings authenticate canonical leaves of the same words. Conditional on those words, the distinct-query error for at most a−1 passing positions is bounded by choose(a−1,87)/choose(N,87).

Let the initial weight at each original position be 1 if the committed DEEP word equals the virtual word there and 0 otherwise. Before each arity-eight fold challenge, the previous weights and the current word have already been fixed. Push their weights forward to the next domain by averaging each group of eight. After the next word is committed, keep that mean only where the folded value equals the next word. On the final domain the verifier compares with the announced polynomial. Summing over the eight-to-one fibers gives final mean weight |P|/N by induction. This construction accounts for the verifier checking the selected slot of a committed leaf.

An arity-eight word on a coset decomposes uniquely as the sum of x^c f_c(x⁸) for c from 0 through 7, by interpolation on each fiber. The three binary folds inside `fold_leaf` with challenges b, b² and b⁴ yield the corresponding power curve in b. The weighted curve theorem bounds the probability that this curve gains agreement with the next Reed–Solomon code unless all eight coefficient words already agree on a common set with the same weight. Recombining the eight low-degree polynomials on the original fibers preserves their weight, and their maximum degree is one below the incoming code dimension. At fold i the mean weights have common denominator M=8^(i+1). The native fold geometry keeps the rate at 1/8, including the last fold. Substituting the folded domain length d, curve degree 7 and M into **both** terms of equation (7.1) bounds the challenge error. [Ben-Sasson, Carmon, Ishai, Kopparty and Saraf, Theorem 7.2, p. 32](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10467091)

At the first word, agreement at initial weight implies agreement with the virtual word on original rows. The virtual word is one degree-(B−1) power curve over pointwise difference quotients, where B is the number of OOD items. The committed rows, OOD positions and claimed values predate the batching challenge. `DeepLayout::coefficients` generates one sequence of powers across both OOD points. Theorem 6.2 therefore supplies one common set of at least a positions for all B quotient words, except with the stated curve error. For each item, reconstruct the source polynomial as (X−z)t(X)+v, or use its shifted point. The two reconstructed polynomials for a repeated source agree with that same column at more than n points and have degree at most n, so they coincide. Values in base-field columns on the common set also force all non-base coefficients to vanish, since all the original domain points lie in the base field. The resulting tuple projects to the earlier joint lists. [Theorem 6.2, p. 28](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10467091)

For a curve of degree ℓ on a domain of d points and field of size Q, the unweighted bound used here is ℓ(m+½)⁷d²/(3ρ³ᐟ²Q). For weights of common denominator M, the bound is the larger of that quantity and ℓ(2m+1)(Md+1)/(√ρ Q). In the native eight-car case Q=(2⁶⁴−2³²+1)⁴, ρ=1/8 and the DEEP curve has degree 1,614 on 65,536 points. The three fold curves have degree seven on 8,192, 1,024 and 128 points with M equal to 8, 64 and 512 respectively. Their separate conditional error estimates are 175.238, 181.238 and 187.238 bits. The table reports the weakest of the three. [Published curve bounds, equations (6.1) and (7.1)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10467091)

## Prefix errors and scope

The native main commitment and integer multiplicities precede both lookup challenges. Conditional on a candidate in the joint list, the two rational discrepancies share a single cyclic-accumulator check. An incorrect discrepancy, or a pole at a candidate witness value, is covered by the combined root-count and pole bound. The auxiliary commitment precedes the separate composition challenge. The verifier assigns consecutive powers to constraints, the accumulator identity and the helper identities. A tuple that violates a required identity on H then has an exceptional composition challenge with probability at most (C−1)/Q, before the list factor. The quotient commitment precedes z. Low and high chunks recombine in powers of X^n, with the low transition sum multiplied by X minus the final row point. An invalid reassembled identity has a bounded-degree residual. The two-residual bound in the linked calculation conservatively accounts for both groups. The verifier rejects excluded OOD points and does not take credit for proof-of-work grinding. ## Concrete compiler review

A third Claude Fable 5.2 xhigh review considered the actual chained transcript hash, counter-based expansion, shared Merkle openings and the final grinding nonce. Under a framed 384-bit random oracle, the proposed reduction logs primitive queries, charges digest collisions and premature references separately, and samples each complete challenge stream at its first use. Consecutive lookup squeezes must be charged together because no prover message intervenes and the lookup bound is for their joint event. The weight arguments above treat partial oracle commitments as words with erasures. A fixed completion chosen at commitment time preserves every valid opening. It can enlarge the set of passing positions, so it gives an upper bound on acceptance.

Two details matter before turning this outline into a concrete security claim. First, the 8,192-row native instance has three FRI folds. Second, a bound using total prover **plus verifier** oracle calls does not immediately give the project's work-factor bound measured only in adversarial calls. Rejection sampling also gives the verifier a variable number of hash calls. An audit needs an explicit bound on that tail and a separate accounting for the final verification path. For example, at the smallest enumerated domain the chance of drawing a duplicate before 87 distinct indices have been collected is at most 86/2,048 per draw. Failure to finish within 1,024 draws requires at least 938 duplicates. A union bound gives less than 2⁻²⁷²⁸ per fresh stream. The field-limb sampler rejects with probability below 2⁻³² per draw. These bounds address the sampling tail; they do not establish a state-restoration theorem for the concrete verifier.

The implementation review checked framing, index and depth binding, full sibling consumption, the unique coefficient exponents in both OOD groups, the three fold geometries and the shared lookup transcript order. The remaining deliverable is an independently checked, adversary-budgeted reduction tying those implementation facts to every prefix state, including commitments with unopened leaves. The modeled oracle assumption and the correctness of the circuit relative to the race rules must be stated separately. ### A budget for one submitted proof

For one fixed false engine statement, the native verifier authenticates three depth-16 trace trees and FRI trees of depths 13, 10 and 7. For a tree of depth d, its multiproof uses at most Σ min(87, 2ʲ) hashes over levels 0 through d. Across six trees the bound is 4,416. There are 35 fixed transcript and framing calls. Allow three 48-byte blocks per field challenge and 24 for the final indices. The probability of exhausting one such window is bounded by C(18,15)((2³²−1)/2⁶⁴)¹⁵ for the field sampler and by C(144,58)(86/65,536)⁵⁸ for the index sampler. Thus a native verification takes at most 4,499 framed oracle calls outside an explicit tail event. The implementation continues sampling beyond these analytical cutoffs.

Let T count the adversary’s framed random-oracle queries and E be the sum of the eight conditional round errors. Assigning each pair of lookup streams together, one proof submitted to the verifier adds at most one candidate prefix per round. In the proposed model, δ is eight field tails plus one index tail and S = 32(T+4,499) reserves the possible stream blocks. The proposed false-acceptance bound is (T+1)(E+δ) + 4(S+1)²/2³⁸⁴. With E below 2⁻¹³⁰·¹⁴⁹⁶ and 1 ≤ T ≤ 2¹²⁸, the lower bound on −log₂(probability/T) is 129.149 bits, about 1.149 bits above the target under these assumptions. The calculation assumes one fixed statement, one proof submission and complete length-framed BLAKE2b-384 calls modeled as random-oracle queries. It does not measure the full deployed signing or ledger protocol. ### Checking the two native constraint groups

A fourth Claude Fable 5.2 xhigh review considered the compiled AIR and the tuple that the verifier recovers after the batching challenge. A tuple agreeing on at least 23,264 rows projects to the previous lists on those same rows. The union at an earlier challenge therefore covers every later-selected tuple. On all 24 native shapes, the range and fixed-table lookups read only the current row. All rotated main-column reads appear in the DEEP layout. The low and high groups have degrees three and five, with two and four quotient chunks. Even allowing the reconstructed polynomials degree n, the residuals have degrees at most 24,577 and 40,960. Their combined bound is 232.173 bits. Counting all lookup occurrences and table rows preserves the earlier lookup bound. This checks the two-group projection under the ideal commitments and weighted DEEP argument. A complete release review must also establish that satisfying the frozen circuit implements the integer controller and that the ledger adapter binds its outcome and custody effects to the intended race.
