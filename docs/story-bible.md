# SORA CARS — 1986: Side B of the sky

An original alternate-history racing romance. Aki Soramoto tries to save her late father's garage without depending on anyone. A threatened courier depot, a borrowed tool and a promise she cannot keep alone draw her into a larger fight. The ending protects a working neighborhood and opens a relationship; ordinary debt, repair work and public accountability continue.

This is the narrative canon for the twelve chapters in `src/game/story-campaign-act1.ts`, `story-campaign-act2.ts` and `story-campaign-act3.ts`. IDs below are zero-based; the player sees chapters 1–12. Japanese is the original dialogue and English the subtitle.

## The conflict

The fictional **Meridian Council** (メリディアン評議会) and its Japanese development partners operate **CROWN** (クラウン計画). They expand property lending, coordinate withdrawals, acquire distressed sites and make work and payments depend on their permissioned clearing network. Celeste Vale knowingly pursues that control. Toru Amamiya believes concentrated resources can prevent the factory closure that scarred his family. Rei Sorashiro approved assessments that omitted the costs borne by existing residents and workers.

The garage has enough booked repair work for normal installments, but cannot repay the entire loan within the new three-week deadline. One broken lift also limits capacity. More van inspections help keep the business running; they cannot by themselves solve the accelerated demand. Evidence, a lender review and the public hearing address that demand separately from any race result.

**SORA X.O.R.** is the crew's fictional, retrofuturist exchange network. Shops verify their exchange records, retain copies and eventually receive shared designs and printed recovery instructions. Participation is voluntary; declining the exchange does not bar someone from using the workshop. It is part of this alternate 1986, not a claim about real monetary history or an automatic solution to the garage's debt.

## People and change

- **Aki, 24:** a mechanic who mistakes isolation for her father's strength. She asks for help, relinquishes the recovery key she kept, refuses to rescue only her own garage and makes room for customers and friends.
- **Ren, 23:** a DJ afraid that silence will make him unwanted. He conceals a sponsor deal connected to his mother's debt, harms the relay hosts and Aki, then answers questions, publishes corrected costs and accepts boundaries. Work and affection do not purchase forgiveness.
- **Mei, 26:** sold an eviction photograph after leaving a distressed shopkeeper outside the frame. She contacts the displaced shopkeeper, keeps her own role in the account and lets people choose photographs and correct captions.
- **Daichi, 25:** supports his brother and tries to carry every delivery himself. He shares routes and duties, and accepts that his brother can choose what happens to his own car. Driving school comes before the brother drives it himself.
- **Yui, 27:** wants to build useful, elegant machines but initially keeps the repair knowledge to herself. Other people's corrections, readable receipts and accessible recovery procedures improve the design.
- **Jun, 28:** repairs other people's instruments while hiding his abandoned song. The conversation about Aki's father's notebook helps her ask for help; Jun gradually returns to his own music and records the completed piece for the sunrise programme.
- **Rei, 29:** exposes the assessment bearing her signature, loses her position and continues answering for her omissions. Her investigation remains open after the neighborhood's victory.
- **Toru, 34:** starts to recognize that protecting his father's memory has meant threatening other people's workshops. His personal race wager still reflects pride; he admits that limitation before driving and keeps his promise afterward.
- **Celeste, 32:** offers precisely what each person wants, while retaining control over the terms. Her project is suspended locally; neither she nor the wider Council disappears after one defeat.

## Continuity anchors

**Time and debt.** ID 0 begins in early August 1986 with a three-week repayment demand. ID 4 moves forward one week. The deadline is approaching during IDs 5–7; after ID 7, the lender has examined the originals and granted a standstill. Only then does ID 8 advance another week. ID 10 reports the lender's separate withdrawal of the accelerated demand. Original installments continue, funded by repair income and reserves. ID 11 occurs several days later, during reopening preparations.

**The blackout.** Ren obtained mobile relay kits from the sponsor and lent them onward to shops. The hosts did not know the agreement's connection to his mother's debt, its content-approval terms or his disclosure of their locations. His list enables coordinated collection at two a.m. The borrowed power unit in the garage supplies both its relay and the work lamps; recovering that unit darkens the work area. This is a physical equipment dependency, not the sponsor ordering an unexplained municipal blackout. Distributed records and copies of the CROWN evidence survive. Ren had time to confess during the shared drive home and explicitly owns his decision to remain silent.

**The cassette.** Ren gives Aki the cassette for her new sign in ID 0. Side A has music; Side B is blank. She leaves it in his keeping during ID 4, before their planned date, because he has not yet chosen his one song for Side B. The rain is a shared memory, never an earlier recording. The cassette remains with Ren through the rupture. Aki takes it back before the last Nikko trip and chooses when to record. Their first kiss is consensual and private; only afterward does she press Record, beginning with being hungry.

**The final day.** The crew prepares to reopen and records Jun's finished song in the morning before leaving for Nikko. A shop sitter plays the recorded programme at the following dawn. The aftermath briefly shows the previous morning's studio recording with explicit bilingual setting captions, then returns to Aki and Ren at the Nikko overlook. They travel home afterward, repair the leak and reopen the garage that afternoon. The studio performance is not simultaneous live activity in Yokohama by people who are also at Nikko.

## Races and presentation

All chapters require a podium. ID 10 additionally requires finishing ahead of Toru; no dialogue may assume first place when third satisfies the objective. Races provide different opportunities: initial publicity, a place to meet shopkeepers, record handoffs, rebuilding friendships, Toru's personal challenge and a final celebration. Essential evidence and permission to ask questions never depend on winning a microphone.

The campaign has 298 authored chapter lines plus three retry lines. Preserve deliberate camera and music directions when editing. The current presentation uses 54 cutscene illustrations and a 25-composition soundtrack; see [artwork](cutscene-art.md), [music](cutscene-music.md) and [implementation design](story-campaign-design.md). Time/place captions mark phone calls, the chapter 2 flashback, montage cuts, time jumps and the recorded programme whenever the artwork alone could be ambiguous; each holds until the next camera cut. Locked chapters conceal their synopses, and dossiers unlock after completion.

Story progress is local gameplay. It does not mint tokens, award NFTs or prove a live network deployment. Optional modern workshop purchases remain separate, explicit wallet-approved transactions; cosmetics do not pay fictional debts, repair relationships or alter race performance.
